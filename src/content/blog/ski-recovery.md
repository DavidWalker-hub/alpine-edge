## Why Recovery is the missing piece in Ski Performance

At Alpine Edge, we believe better skiing is built through more than just time on the slopes. Recovery plays a critical role in performance, and what we do off snow can be just as important as what we do on snow.

A typical ski holiday often involves, long, unstructured days on the mountain followed by alcohol, a heavy meal, poor sleep and very little physical recovery. While that can be part of the appeal and a great way to experience a ski resort, it also explains why so many skiers feel like they aren’t improving, feeling exhausted and stiff by the middle of the week.

Our approach is designed differently. At Alpine Edge we recognise that recovery has major impact on skiing performance. By balancing social evenings and experiences guests are able to enjoy the week while still maintaining energy, focus, and strength needed to make positive changes to their skiing.

## Fatigue Changes How You Ski

Skiing is highly demanding physically and neurologically. Strong skiing relies on balance, coordination, reaction time and precise movement patterns. As fatigue builds, these systems become less efficient.

This is often why technique deteriorates later in the day or later in the week. Combine this with the fact it can often take skiers a few runs or even a couple of days to feel like they are skiing at a similar level to their previous best (often a year ago for many skiers) and its not difficult to imagine why many feel as though they have reached a plateau in their ability.

- Energy Production

When recovery improves, skiing will too.

## Mobility and Movement Quality

Mobility Training offers more than traditional stretching. Practices such as Controlled Articular Rotations (CARS) and Kinstretch are designed to maintain and expand the usable range of motion available at your joints and will improve joint health, movement capacity and body awareness. The effect could be hugely beneficial to skiers and will translate to better balance, more efficient movement patterns, improved edge control and greater confidence in a variety of terrain.

These practises can also play an important role in recovery. Gentle, controlled movement helps reduce stiffness, improves circulation, and restores movement quality after long days of skiing. Beyond the immediate benefits on the mountain, many people find that regular mobility work improves overall movement, reduces everyday aches and pains, and creates a greater understanding of how their body moves and performs.

The goal is not simply to become more flexible, but to build a body that moves well, recovers effectively and is better prepared for the physical demands of skiing, and life.

## Heat and Cold Exposure

Saunas, hot tubs and ice baths have become increasingly popular in athletic recovery, for good reason.

Heat Exposure can:

- Improve Circulation
- Promote Relaxation
- Reduce Muscular Tightness
- Support Recovery
- Improve Sleep

Cold Exposure can help reduce soreness and inflammation while also helping some athletes feel more refreshed and alert.

Used correctly, both can become valuable tools for maintaining energy and physical readiness during a demanding week of skiing. Additionally, these moments provide an opportunity to slow down, switch off from the demands of daily life, and enjoy the social side of the mountains. At Alpine Edge, recovery is not viewed as another box to tick, but an experience that helps guests feel refreshed, connected and ready for the next day on the mountain.
